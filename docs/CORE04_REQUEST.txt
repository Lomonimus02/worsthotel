You are continuing development of the EXISTING Unity project:

THE WORST HOTEL EVER

Do NOT create a new project.
Do NOT rebuild existing working systems unless a specific architectural change is required by this milestone.

This is the next major core milestone:

PROTOTYPE 0.4 — THE WORST HOTEL CORE
CONTINUOUS OPERATIONS + CAPACITY PRESSURE

==================================================
0. WHY THIS MILESTONE EXISTS
==================================================

Recent full three-day playtests revealed a deeper core-design problem.

The game currently contains several individually promising systems:

- guests now behave more believably;
- boiler failures can create good physical gameplay;
- electrical overload can create useful decisions;
- temperature problems can connect guests to infrastructure;
- room keys / housekeeping / physical items provide hotel atmosphere;
- guest service has improved.

However, the overall fantasy still often feels like:

"A normal functioning hotel where something occasionally breaks."

This is NOT the intended core fantasy.

The intended fantasy is:

"We inherited an old hotel that technically works,
but its infrastructure is slightly weaker than the business
we are trying to run through it.

We constantly decide how hard to push it."

The hotel should NOT require constant arbitrary failures.

The hotel should NOT contain a list of fake permanent defects
whose only purpose is to give the player quests.

Instead:

THE BUILDING HAS REAL LIMITS.

Players choose how close to those limits they operate.

The key fantasy:

"Technically, it still works."

==================================================
1. CORE DESIGN CHANGE
==================================================

The new core loop should become:

OBSERVE HOTEL

↓

REVIEW UPCOMING BUSINESS

↓

DECIDE HOW MUCH LOAD TO ACCEPT

↓

GUESTS ARRIVE AND USE THE BUILDING

↓

LOAD EXPOSES INFRASTRUCTURE LIMITS

↓

PLAYER CHOOSES:
- tolerate;
- redistribute;
- patch;
- maintain;
- repair;
- compensate;
- relocate;
- reduce load.

↓

QUIET TIME IS USED TO REDUCE MAINTENANCE DEBT
OR PREPARE FOR FUTURE DEMAND

↓

NEW BUSINESS ARRIVES

↓

REPEAT CONTINUOUSLY.

The game should no longer fundamentally be:

Planning Screen
→ 5-minute Shift
→ Stop
→ Next Day Menu
→ Reset
→ 5-minute Shift.

==================================================
2. HARD RULE — DO NOT SOLVE THIS WITH CHRONIC QUEST DEFECTS
==================================================

Do NOT make the new core:

Room 101 has broken window.
Repair it.

Room 102 has bad pipe.
Repair it.

Room 103 has bad lamp.
Repair it.

If these can simply be fixed in two minutes and forgotten,
they are NOT the core gameplay.

Small faults may continue to exist as texture.

But the actual core should come from:

LIMITED CAPACITY

+

GUEST LOAD

+

EQUIPMENT CONDITION

+

PLAYER AMBITION.

==================================================
3. STRUCTURAL LIMITS, NOT CONSTANT BREAKAGE
==================================================

The hotel should be capable of operating successfully.

However:

the amount of business players WANT to run
should often be greater than the amount the old hotel
can comfortably support.

For Prototype 0.4 tuning:

The hotel physically has 6 guest rooms.

A reasonable initial target could be:

3–4 occupied rooms:
comfortable infrastructure load.

5 occupied rooms:
noticeably strained depending on guest behavior.

6 occupied rooms:
potentially risky during peak usage.

Do NOT hardcode these exact numbers into game logic.

Make them configurable.

The important design principle is:

PHYSICAL ROOM CAPACITY
>
COMFORTABLE INFRASTRUCTURE CAPACITY.

==================================================
PART A — CONTINUOUS HOTEL TIME
==================================================

4. REMOVE HARD SHIFT BOUNDARIES
==================================================

The current structure:

Start Shift
→ countdown
→ Shift Ends
→ world effectively freezes
→ next-day management
→ start next shift

must be replaced.

The hotel should run on ONE CONTINUOUS SIMULATION CLOCK.

There can still be:

calendar days;
morning;
afternoon;
evening;
night.

But:

the hotel itself never magically stops.

Infrastructure never freezes because a shift ended.

Guest state does not reset because a timer reached zero.

==================================================
5. NO MORE FIVE-MINUTE ROUNDS
==================================================

Do not structure gameplay around:

"survive the next 5 minutes."

Instead:

time continues.

Example:

23:48

Boiler fails.

00:00 occurs.

The boiler is STILL FAILED.

Heating continues deteriorating.

Guests remain affected.

At 00:25 the player may still be repairing it.

There is no magical freeze or reset.

==================================================
6. CALENDAR DAY
==================================================

Keep the concept of days only as a BUSINESS / REPORTING unit.

Example:

06:00 or another configurable hotel-day boundary:

previous day's report becomes available.

It may contain:

Revenue
Operating costs
Compensation
Guest reviews
Average occupancy
Maintenance expenses

BUT:

do NOT pause world simulation automatically.

Do NOT force player into a modal menu.

The report may:

appear as a notification;
be reviewed at reception;
be opened later.

==================================================
7. DAILY REPORT IS NOT A LEVEL COMPLETE SCREEN
==================================================

Day transition should feel like:

"another morning arrived."

Not:

"Level 2 complete."

The world continues.

Guest schedules continue.

Infrastructure continues.

Room state continues.

Cash continues.

Maintenance state continues.

15:30 — Guest B — Room 101

TOMORROW

09:00 — Checkout Room 104

Do not require a hard "Start Day" button.

9. APPLICATION ACCEPTANCE

Do not create a separate frozen planning dimension.

Player may use:

11. WAIT / FAST FORWARD

==================================================
PART B — CAPACITY PRESSURE
==================================================

12. HOTEL INFRASTRUCTURE SHOULD HAVE RATED CAPACITY
==================================================

Existing boiler and electrical systems should now communicate:

RATED CAPACITY

CURRENT DEMAND

CURRENT CONDITION

CURRENT STRESS / RESERVE

The player should understand:

"We are close to the limit."

before:

"Everything exploded."

==================================================
13. CAPACITY STATES
==================================================

Use understandable operating states.

For example:

COMFORTABLE

STRAINED

OVERLOADED

CRITICAL

Do not require player to understand engineering formulas.

Debug UI may expose exact numeric values.

Player-facing UI should prioritize readability.

==================================================
14. DO NOT MAKE 100% = INSTANT FAILURE
==================================================

Capacity limits should produce pressure before failure.

Example concept:

Load well below rated capacity:

stable.

Near rated capacity:

reduced reserve;
more wear;
performance may begin dropping.

Above rated capacity:

system cannot fully satisfy demand;
stress accumulates;
wear accelerates.

Sustained severe overload + poor condition:

eventual failure.

Do not immediately explode when demand crosses one threshold.

==================================================
15. SYSTEM STRESS
==================================================

Prefer a causal, inspectable STRESS / OVERLOAD EXPOSURE model.

Example concept:

System stress increases when:

Demand > effective safe capacity.

Stress increases faster when:

equipment condition is poor;
overload is severe.

Stress decreases when:

load returns to safe range.

Failure may occur when:

stress reaches configured critical threshold.

Prefer this over arbitrary:

Random.Range() < FailureChance.

If existing failure system uses randomness:

inspect it.

Do not necessarily remove all randomness,
but the dominant cause should be understandable state.

==================================================
PART C — BOILER
==================================================

16. BOILER ROLE
==================================================

The boiler should be one of the main examples of:

CAPACITY MANAGEMENT.

It has:

RatedHeatingCapacity
Condition
CurrentDemand
CurrentOutput
Stress
FailureState

Guest behavior contributes to demand.

Examples:

radiator settings;
number of occupied rooms;
room heat loss;
guest temperature preferences;
portable heating strategy indirectly affects electricity instead.

==================================================
17. BOILER UNDERLOAD
==================================================

When demand is comfortably within capacity:

rooms heat normally.

Condition degrades slowly.

No reason for boiler failure.

Do NOT force a Day 3 boiler failure.

==================================================
18. BOILER STRAIN
==================================================

When demand approaches / exceeds available output:

not every room necessarily receives ideal heating.

Existing room heat model may determine:

which rooms cool first.

This should naturally create:

guest discomfort.

No scripted ColdGuestEvent is required.

==================================================
19. CONDITION AFFECTS PERFORMANCE
==================================================

Boiler condition should matter.

Poor condition can affect:

effective capacity;
efficiency;
stress accumulation;
reliability.

Keep effects configurable and readable.

Do NOT make 50% condition literally mean:
50% output
unless intentionally tuned.

==================================================
PART D — ELECTRICAL SYSTEM
==================================================

20. ELECTRICAL CIRCUITS ARE CAPACITY CONSTRAINTS
==================================================

Existing electrical circuits should remain simple.

Each has:

RatedCapacity
CurrentLoad
OverloadDuration / Stress

Consumers include actual supported systems.

Examples:

occupied room baseline;
portable heater;
other existing devices.

==================================================
21. BREAKER IS NOT A RANDOM FAILURE
==================================================

The breaker should trip because:

load exceeded circuit capacity for long enough.

Resetting breaker is fast.

That is fine.

The gameplay question is:

"Why is this circuit overloaded?"

not:

"Can I perform a 30-second breaker minigame?"

==================================================
22. RESET DOES NOT SOLVE CAPACITY
==================================================

If:

Circuit capacity = 6

Load = 7

Player resets breaker.

Circuit should eventually trip again.

Player must change load or upgrade infrastructure.

25. STRUCTURAL IMPROVEMENTS MAY EXIST LATER

For Prototype 0.4:

PART F — MAINTENANCE DEBT
==================================================

26. QUIET TIME SHOULD HAVE PURPOSE
==================================================

When no guest currently needs attention,
the player should still be able to improve their future situation.

Introduce / strengthen:

MAINTENANCE BACKLOG.

This is NOT a quest list.

It is information about the current state of the building.

Example:

BOILER
Condition: 58%
Recommended maintenance

CIRCUIT B
Frequent overload

Room 104
High heating demand

Room 106
Needs turnover

Players decide what matters.

==================================================
27. MAINTENANCE IS OPTIONAL UNTIL IT IS NOT
==================================================

Do not force:

"Perform boiler maintenance now."

Player may ignore maintenance.

That is a valid risk.

But:

condition remains poor;
stress builds faster;
future load becomes harder to manage.

==================================================
28. THREE LEVELS OF INFRASTRUCTURE RESPONSE
==================================================

Separate:

A. EMERGENCY PATCH

B. PROPER MAINTENANCE / REPAIR

C. CAPITAL UPGRADE

These must mean different things.

==================================================
29. EMERGENCY PATCH
==================================================

Purpose:

get failed infrastructure running again quickly.

Characteristics:

fast;
relatively cheap;
restores operation;
does NOT fully restore condition;
may leave a reliability penalty / degraded state.

Example:

Boiler failed.

Emergency patch:

restores function at 35–50% condition.

Exact values configurable.

This is appropriate during crisis.

==================================================
30. PROPER MAINTENANCE / REPAIR
==================================================

Purpose:

restore existing equipment.

Characteristics:

takes longer;
costs more;
may require temporary shutdown;
restores significant condition;
removes temporary patch penalty.

IMPORTANT:

A proper repair must feel meaningful.

Do NOT make the same failure return 5 minutes later
just because the game needs content.

If properly repaired:

the player has genuinely improved the hotel.

==================================================
31. CAPITAL UPGRADE
==================================================

Purpose:

increase what the hotel is capable of.

Example:

OLD BOILER

Rated capacity:
X

UPGRADED BOILER

Rated capacity:
X + meaningful amount.

Likewise:

Circuit B rewiring / upgrade
may increase circuit capacity.

This is not maintenance.

This is PROGRESSION.

==================================================
32. UPGRADE CREATES ROOM FOR MORE AMBITION
==================================================

Core progression principle:

Player solves old bottleneck.

↓

Hotel becomes easier at current scale.

↓

Player accepts more business / later opens more hotel.

↓

New bottleneck appears.

Do NOT artificially make repaired systems worse again.

Growth should create new pressure.

==================================================
33. PROTOTYPE 0.4 UPGRADE SCOPE
==================================================

Do NOT build a full upgrade tree.

Implement only enough to prove the concept.

Recommended:

ONE boiler capacity upgrade.

ONE electrical capacity upgrade.

Both:

expensive enough to matter;
persistent;
clearly improve system capacity.

No skill trees.

No 20 upgrade nodes.

==================================================
34. MAINTENANCE DOWNTIME
==================================================

Proper maintenance may require system downtime.

Example:

Boiler maintenance:

heating unavailable for a configured in-game duration.

This creates planning:

Do it now while occupancy is low?

Or keep operating and risk failure?

Keep implementation understandable.

==================================================
PART H — GUEST LOAD
==================================================

40. GUESTS ARE DEMAND GENERATORS
==================================================

Examples:

taking shower:

more heating demand.

portable heater:
more electrical load.

Do NOT create invisible fake demand solely for balance.

==================================================
41. GUEST TRAITS AFFECT LOAD INDIRECTLY
==================================================

Examples:

ColdSensitive:

prefers warmer room;
may increase radiator sooner;
may request blanket;
therefore indirectly increases heating pressure.

BusinessGuest:

more noise sensitive;
may value reliable electricity more.

NoisyGuest:

creates room assignment conflicts.

This makes booking decisions meaningful.

Recent playtesting exposed an architecture / behavior problem.

A guest may:

go shower;
sleep;
leave hotel.

This creates absurd behavior.

Example:

Guest asks for room change.

↓

Immediately leaves hotel.

↓

Guest Service

and

Guest Schedule

This must be corrected.

==================================================
43. SERVICE INTENT
==================================================

Introduce a concept similar to:

ServiceIntent

or an equivalent architecture.

Each active service request must declare
what interaction relationship it requires.

At minimum:

REMOTE

DROP_OFF

DIRECT

==================================================
44. REMOTE SERVICE
==================================================

REMOTE means:

guest does not need to remain available.

Examples:

Room is cold.
Power problem.
Noise source affecting room.

Guest may:

continue activity;
leave room;
sometimes leave hotel.

Player can fix underlying hotel condition.

When guest returns / perception updates,
service case may resolve.

==================================================
45. DROP_OFF SERVICE
==================================================

DROP_OFF means:

guest requested a physical item,
but player does not need to chase them.

Example:

Extra blanket.

Guest may say:

"Could you leave one for me?"

Provide a clear delivery location.

Examples:

outside-room service point;
room delivery spot if access is permitted.

The item must physically arrive there.

When guest next returns / becomes available,
they consume / acknowledge delivery.

Do NOT require catching moving guest NPC.

==================================================
46. DIRECT SERVICE
==================================================

DIRECT means:

guest interaction requires guest presence.

Examples:

Room change.
Compensation discussion.
Late checkout decision.
Certain front-desk requests.

When Direct Service becomes active:

GuestSchedule MUST respect it.

Guest enters an appropriate state:

WaitingAtReception
WaitingInRoom
WaitingForStaff

for a reasonable amount of time.

They do NOT immediately:

take shower;
sleep;
leave hotel.

==================================================
47. DIRECT SERVICE TIMEOUT
==================================================

Guests should not wait forever.

If ignored:

after configured time:

guest may:
- become frustrated;
- abandon request;
- continue schedule;
- escalate later.

But the schedule must understand
that the guest was waiting.

==================================================
48. REQUEST GENERATION MUST CHECK SCHEDULE CONTEXT
==================================================

Do NOT generate ordinary service requests
at obviously incompatible moments.

Examples:

Do not generate extra blanket request:
while guest is leaving hotel in 10 seconds.

Do not generate late-checkout conversation:
while guest is showering.

Do not create ordinary service contact:
while guest is asleep.

Instead:

defer evaluation until a sensible activity window.

==================================================

==================================================
