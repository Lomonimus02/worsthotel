Evidence is `Logs/p02-phase9-compile.log`, `Logs/p02-phase9-editmode.xml`, `Logs/p02-phase9-playmode.xml` and the extracted corrected trace `Logs/living-emergence.txt`. ## Production scenario

LivingEmergenceTests.cs reads PrototypeSession.asset and its referenced living, needs, noise, heater, electricity and housekeeping configurations. It takes the seed and thermal time constant from those assets. It never forces a failure or an activity, changes room temperature/noise, overrides boiler load, or supplies replacement tuning.

Each run first plays Day 1 with the four actual applications: the budget guests use rooms 102 and 106, the cold-sensitive guest uses 101, and the business guest uses 105. Staff give each arrival the configured check-in hold. The entire shift settles, maintenance is explicitly deferred, old guests depart, and the one worker prepares the rooms through ordinary callbacks. The next day therefore inherits actual wear and room temperatures.

Day 2 accepts the first five ordinary applications at reference prices. The plan places a budget guest in 101, a cold-sensitive guest in 104, a business guest in 106, another budget guest in 105 and the other cold-sensitive guest in 102. No special complaint is requested. If the guest in 104 develops an actual cold-temperature complaint, the staff policy chooses a portable heater after a declared 12-second transport delay and leaves it switched on. The electrical model alone decides whether and when its normal load warns or trips.

The comparison runs use the same offers, prices, seed and first-day policy. One moves the budget booking from 105 to 103 while using the same heater policy; the other retains the original bookings but omits the heater. Both 105→106 and 103→106 are authored corridor sound links, which reduces an avoidable acoustic difference in the placement comparison. Real differences in route length and later activity timing are retained, not edited out.

The test records shower activities, peak boiler load, minimum heating output, cold-complaint time, heater placement temperature, powered warming, temperature dissatisfaction, overload warning/trip, secondary room-condition complaint, actual power-loss exposure, reviews, refunds and settlement. It passed the checks that the heater warms a real room before a natural trip and that the business guest's electrical complaint follows actual power-loss exposure. Both declining the added device and changing one booking's circuit prevented the electrical failure in this seeded case.

## Measured causal chain

All three runs use seed 1947 and the actual 45-second thermal time constant. Their normal schedules produce twelve shower activities and a maximum boiler demand of 6.57. The previous four-guest day ends without boiler failure; deferring maintenance carries condition 62.0276 into the second day. No coefficient was changed to make this trace pass.

Moving the booking keeps power available, but an unattended heater then overheats its room: the measured peak 30.42188°C exceeds this cold-sensitive guest's preferred band. Its final room temperature is 25.45899°C. The switch remains a real ongoing decision; there is no hidden thermostat. Omitting the heater avoids the secondary electrical damage and has the best total net in this deliberately unattended comparison, while the original cold room ends at 12.07183°C. Neither counterfactual solves every guest's problem. The secondary guest also suffers earlier cold/noise; reviews correctly summarize those leading exposures rather than necessarily naming power loss among their top two issues.

The initial XML's broad per-request text trace used a sorted request-list index and could repeat the wrong room/cause when another key was inserted. The numeric first-event fields, direct causal assertions, temperatures and receipts above do not use that indexing. The logger now subscribes to `OnRequestCreated` and unsubscribes in `finally`, preserving model/actions/assertions. Those request lines label the tick-start time; the measured first-observation fields use tick-end time, at a 0.2-second production tick. The final gate passed with the corrected diagnostic log, extracted to `Logs/living-emergence.txt`.

## Boundary-adapter limits

These are headless simulation runs. They preserve nonzero arrival and room travel using the authored path dimensions and guest speed, two separate staff slots with the configured check-in hold, departure clearance time, one worker's travel and the actual configured cleaning duration. Only successful public boundary commands admit or release guests and start cleaning. They do not directly set occupancy, dirt, guest state or task progress.

The timed callbacks are an explicit navigation adapter, not a collision simulation or proof that a person carried the heater in twelve seconds. Prior PlayMode gates cover real guest door routes, check-in input, heater carrying/placement, circuit reset, departure clearance and worker travel. Human timing, interruption and path obstruction can change when the natural chain occurs.

The staff policy waits for an actual complaint before choosing an ordinary tool. This conditional test driver is not a production sequence controller: it does not direct the boiler, thermal model, electrical circuit or secondary guest to take predetermined steps. All three runs continue to the ordinary end of the shift, even after the measured chain.

## Acceptance scope

The passed final trace provides evidence for a real decision connecting multiple existing systems, a consequential room assignment, useful supplemental heat, actual electrical demand and a later complaint caused by power loss. The omission run separates the heater's added consequence from unrelated problems already present in a busy hotel. The results demonstrate a tradeoff, not an assertion that all metrics improve when staff use a heater.

This case does not, on its own, prove that all three days are balanced, that early safe play earns less than risky play, that players find the choice understandable, or that the heater improves happiness before every possible trip. [PROTOTYPE02_BALANCE.md](PROTOTYPE02_BALANCE.md) records the separate measured Day 1–3 policy comparison, independent concurrent causes, event gaps and turnover durations. The brief's questions about conversation, regret, strategic variety, an alive-feeling hotel and housekeeping not dominating player attention require a human two-player session. Real-controller ergonomics, audio mix and real-display performance also remain manual.

The previous Phase 7 controlled thermal/electrical diagnostic remains valid and separate. This executed production-config natural trace supplies the Phase 9 cross-system evidence; the Phase 10 final regression/build and human play are distinct remaining gates.
