==================================================
1. ROOM KEYS — REMOVE HOLD-E CHECK-IN
==================================================

The current check-in interaction is not satisfying.

Currently, issuing a room key effectively behaves like:

Hold E
→ wait several seconds
→ guest receives key.

Remove this.

There should be an actual physical source of room keys.

Create a KEY RACK behind or beside the reception desk.

Example:

101
102
103
104
105
106

Each occupied room has a corresponding physical key.

The key should be:

- a small but clearly readable physical object;
- preferably an old hotel key with a large numbered brass/plastic tag;
- grabbable through the existing physics interaction system;
- visually associated with its room number.

CHECK-IN FLOW:

Guest arrives.

↓

Guest waits at reception.

↓

Player checks the guest's assigned room.

↓

Player physically takes the correct room key from the rack.

↓

Player hands / gives the key to the guest.

↓

Guest proceeds to the room.

This interaction should take seconds, not 10 seconds of holding a button.

The interesting part is:

finding / grabbing / giving the correct key.

Not waiting for a progress bar.

Do not make this intentionally tedious.

The rack must be immediately accessible from reception.

==================================================

Keys should have actual game state.

Example:

Room 103 Key:
OnRack
HeldByPlayer
HeldByGuest

When a guest checks out:

the key should eventually return to reception.

For Prototype purposes this can be simple.

Do NOT yet add:

lost keys;
duplicate keys;
key cutting;
master keys;
key theft.

These may become future systems.

Right now the important thing is:

the key exists in the world.

==================================================
3. REMOVE THE STARTING HOUSEKEEPER
==================================================

Remove / disable the automatic housekeeper from the early prototype.

The players should perform basic hotel work themselves at the beginning of the game.

This is intentional.

Do not automatically clean rooms for them.

During the early game, the two hotel owners ARE the staff.

This includes:

- preparing rooms;
- changing bed linen;
- dealing with basic trash;
- resetting rooms after checkout;
- basic hotel maintenance;
- reception;
- responding to guests.

NPC employees should become a FUTURE PROGRESSION REWARD.

The fantasy should eventually become:

"We used to do all of this ourselves.
Now the hotel is large enough that we can afford staff."

Do NOT implement employee progression yet.

Simply remove the starting employee.

==================================================
4. ROOM TURNOVER — PLAYER CHORES
==================================================

After checkout:

Room state becomes:

DIRTY / NOT READY.

The room cannot accept another normal guest until it is prepared.

However:

DO NOT turn this into a cleaning simulator.

Avoid:

20 individual trash objects;
scrubbing every surface;
clicking five stains;
repetitive pixel hunting.

The room preparation process should be tactile but concise.

Prototype room turnover should require approximately:

A. REMOVE DIRTY LINEN

Player physically removes dirty sheets / linen bundle.

B. DISPOSE / DELIVER DIRTY LINEN

Dirty linen goes into a laundry hamper / dirty linen cart / chute.

C. GET CLEAN LINEN

Clean linen exists physically in a linen closet or storage shelf.

D. MAKE BED

Bring clean linen to bed.

Perform a short contextual interaction.

Do NOT use a 10-second Hold E progress bar.

A short 1–2 second interaction / animation is acceptable.

E. BASIC ROOM RESET

Optional one simple task such as:
- remove trash bag;
- replace one consumable;
- reset obvious room disorder.

Then:

Room → READY.

==================================================
5. WHY THESE CHORES EXIST
==================================================

These chores are not random quests.

They come from hotel operation.

Guest stayed in room.

↓

Guest checked out.

↓

Room is dirty.

↓

Room cannot be sold again.

↓

Players must decide when it is worth spending time preparing it.

This is MANAGEMENT CAPACITY.

It should matter especially when:

Room 104 is dirty

but

a valuable guest arrives in 3 minutes.

Now cleaning Room 104 is meaningful.

Do NOT create:

"Random task: Change sheets in Room 104."

The room itself must generate the need.

==================================================
6. LINEN SHOULD COME FROM SOMEWHERE
==================================================

Just like room keys:

clean sheets should NOT magically appear through an interaction.

Create a small LINEN STORAGE area.

It can initially be extremely simple:

shelf with clean linen bundles.

Clean linen:
finite physical bundles during a shift.

Dirty linen:
goes into a hamper.

For now:

Do NOT implement a full industrial laundry simulation.

At the end / start of a day the clean stock may refill automatically.

Later this can become a real laundry system.

The important Prototype 0.2 principle is:

PHYSICAL ITEMS SHOULD HAVE A SOURCE.

Keys come from the key rack.

Clean linen comes from linen storage.

Portable heater comes from utility storage.

Do not spawn gameplay objects directly into player hands.

==================================================
7. NOISE COMPLAINTS CURRENTLY HAVE POOR COUNTERPLAY
==================================================

Currently a noise complaint appears,
but the obvious response seems to be:

Offer Money.

This is not enough.

A noise complaint should have several valid responses.

Example:

Room 102:
Business Guest
Noise complaint.

Source:
Room 103
Noisy Guest.

Players should be able to:

A. TALK TO THE NOISY GUEST

Go to Room 103.

Knock / interact.

Ask them to keep the noise down.

This should reduce NoiseOutput significantly.

But it may be temporary.

A particularly noisy guest may become loud again later.

Do not make this a long dialogue system.

Simple contextual interaction:

"Ask to keep it down"

is enough.

------------------------------

B. MOVE THE COMPLAINING GUEST

If another suitable room is available:

reassign guest.

Need:
new room;
new room key.

This should immediately change the simulation.

------------------------------

C. MOVE THE NOISY GUEST

If possible.

May be less attractive if the noisy guest is tolerant but moving them costs time.

------------------------------

D. OFFER COMPENSATION

Existing solution.

Money exchanged for reduced dissatisfaction / patience recovery.

This should NOT erase the actual noise source.

If noise continues,
the guest may become unhappy again.

------------------------------

E. IGNORE IT

Take the satisfaction / review consequence.

This is a valid management decision.

==================================================
8. NOISE MUST HAVE AN UNDERLYING SOURCE
==================================================

Do not create:

NoiseComplaint
without a meaningful source.

There should be identifiable state such as:

Room 103:
LoudActivity
NoiseOutput = 70

Room 102:
ReceivedNoise = 48

Guest tolerance = 35

Therefore:

complaint.

When the loud guest is asked to quiet down:

NoiseOutput might drop:

70 → 20.

Then the underlying condition improves.

The Situation system should detect this automatically.

Do not manually mark the quest complete.

==================================================
9. MAKE THE SOURCE UNDERSTANDABLE
==================================================

The player currently needs enough information to understand what can be done.

Do NOT necessarily give perfect omniscient information.

But make the problem readable.

Example complaint:

ROOM 102
"There's a lot of noise coming from next door."

This implies:

check adjacent rooms.

When approaching Room 103:

the player should audibly hear music / TV / guests.

This is much better than a UI saying:

QUEST TARGET: ROOM 103.

Use environmental communication.

==================================================
10. NOISE INTERACTION
==================================================

Implement a basic interaction on an occupied noisy room:

Knock.

Guest answers / interaction becomes available.

Option:

ASK TO KEEP IT DOWN.

Result:

current loud activity becomes QuietActivity,
or NoiseOutput receives a large temporary modifier.

Guest personality can affect duration.

For Prototype:

Budget / Noisy guest:
may become noisy again later.

Normal guest:
usually stays quiet.

Do not build dialogue trees.

==================================================
11. CHORES SHOULD BE PHYSICAL — BUT NOT TEDIOUS
==================================================

Use an important design rule:

NO LONG HOLD-E INTERACTIONS.

Holding E may be appropriate for something physically continuous:

turning a valve;
holding a pressure release;
pushing something;
maintaining a mechanical action.

It is NOT appropriate for:

giving someone a key;
changing a room state;
performing paperwork;
cleaning abstractly for ten seconds.

Prefer:

physical object interaction
+
short contextual animation/action.

21. DO NOT OVERDO CHORES
==================================================

Important:

Adding physical chores DOES NOT mean turning the game into chore spam.

For one room turnover:

target approximately 30–60 seconds
for one player once they understand the process.

Not 3 minutes.

Players should become increasingly efficient.

The interesting pressure comes from:

multiple responsibilities competing for their time.

Not from making individual actions painfully slow.

23. DESIGN REMINDER
==================================================

Physical gameplay should answer:

"Because of the hotel state, what do we need to do right now?"

It should NOT answer:

"What random task did the game assign us?"

Good:

"We need Room 104 ready before the next guest arrives."

Bad:

"Quest: Change sheets in Room 104."

Good:

"Guest 102 cannot sleep because 103 is loud."

Bad:

"Quest: Silence guest."

Good:

"We need the Room 105 key because that guest is checking in."

Bad:

"Hold E for 10 seconds to perform check-in."

Protect this distinction throughout implementation.
