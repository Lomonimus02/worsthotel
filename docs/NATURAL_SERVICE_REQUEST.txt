==================================================
PART A
NATURAL GUEST SERVICE
==================================================

1. CURRENT DESIGN PROBLEM
==================================================

Guest behavior is now more varied and believable.

This is good.

However, hotel service still feels artificial.

The current experience can sometimes feel like:

Guest state changes

→ service case appears

→ player solves service case.

This exposes the game system too directly.

We want guests to feel like people first.

The target experience:

Guest lives normally.

↓

Guest experiences something imperfect.

↓

Guest may initially tolerate it
or try a simple response themselves.

↓

If the issue remains relevant,
guest contacts hotel staff naturally.

↓

Player receives information about the problem.

↓

Player chooses what to do.

The service system should feel like:

HOTEL COMMUNICATION

not:

QUEST GENERATION.

==================================================
2. NEW GUEST RESPONSE PIPELINE
==================================================

Guest needs should generally move through:

NORMAL

↓

NOTICE

↓

SELF RESPONSE / TOLERATE

↓

SERVICE REQUEST

↓

COMPLAINT

↓

ESCALATION

Not every need must reach every stage.

Example:

Room becomes slightly cold.

Guest notices.

Guest increases radiator setting.

Room improves.

END.

No service request.

Alternative:

Guest increases radiator.

Room remains cold.

Guest waits.

Then calls reception:

"Sorry, it's still quite cold in here."

Now the hotel becomes involved.

==================================================
3. SELF-HELP
==================================================

Guests should sometimes attempt reasonable self-help before contacting staff.

Keep this lightweight.

Examples:

Mild cold:
guest may raise room radiator setting.

Minor noise:
guest initially tolerates it.

Lamp problem:
guest may temporarily use another available light if room supports it.

Do NOT make guests capable of solving serious infrastructure problems.

They cannot:

repair boiler;
reset electrical circuit;
replace broken equipment;
access maintenance areas.

Self-help exists only to make behavior believable.

==================================================
4. SELF-HELP MUST USE REAL SYSTEMS
==================================================

Do not fake self-help.

Example:

Guest turns radiator higher.

Actual radiator setting changes.

Actual boiler demand changes.

This can create downstream consequences.

Guest self-help therefore participates in the same simulation
as player actions.

==================================================
5. NATURAL TIMING OF REQUESTS
==================================================

Requests should happen at plausible moments.

WAKE-UP CALL:

Preferably requested:
- at check-in;
- during an evening interaction;
- shortly before sleep.

Do NOT randomly generate wake-up call while guest is showering.

------------------------------

LATE CHECKOUT:

Preferably requested:
- during check-in;
- in evening;
- in morning before checkout.

------------------------------

LUGGAGE STORAGE:

Only when:
- arriving before room availability;
- leaving after checkout;
- otherwise contextually relevant.

------------------------------

EXTRA BLANKET:

Only after guest has actually experienced mild cold
for enough simulation time.

------------------------------

NOISE SERVICE REQUEST:

Only after real noise exposure
for a meaningful period.

==================================================
6. COMMUNICATION CHANNELS
==================================================

Do not make every guest need instantly appear as omniscient HUD information.

A guest must communicate with the hotel.

Supported communication methods for this milestone:

A. RECEPTION PHONE

Guest calls reception from room.

B. FRONT DESK

Guest physically approaches reception.

C. KNOCK / ROOM INTERACTION

Player contacts guest at room.

Do not add:
mobile phone app;
text messages;
complex dialogue system.

==================================================
7. RECEPTION PHONE
==================================================

Make the reception phone an important service interface.

When a guest calls:

the physical reception phone rings.

Player can answer.

Only after answering should full request information become known.

Example:

Phone rings.

Player answers.

Guest:

"Hi, sorry. It's still a little cold in here.
Do you have an extra blanket?"

Then:

ServiceCase becomes visible.

This should feel different from:

notification suddenly appearing in HUD.

==================================================
8. MISSED PHONE CALLS
==================================================

Do not make phone calls infinitely annoying.

If player cannot answer immediately:

guest waits.

After configurable duration:

call may stop.

The ServiceCase may remain as:

MISSED CALL / REQUEST WAITING

or guest may try once more later.

Do NOT repeatedly ring every few seconds.

No notification spam.

==================================================
9. FRONT DESK REQUESTS
==================================================

Some guests may physically approach reception.

Examples:

Late checkout.
Luggage storage.
Room change.
Minor service question.

They should:

walk to reception;
wait briefly;
interact with available player.

Do not make every guest request use the same channel.

This variety should help the hotel feel natural.

==================================================
10. REQUEST CONTENT SHOULD BE A PROBLEM, NOT A SOLUTION
==================================================

After communication:

the system should describe what the guest wants / experiences.

Examples:

"It's still cold in my room."

"I can hear someone next door."

"Could I leave my suitcase here?"

"Could I check out later?"

Avoid:

"Bring blanket to Room 104."

"Go silence Guest 103."

"Use heater."

The player determines the solution.

==================================================
11. SERVICE CASES
==================================================

Keep existing persistent ServiceCase system.

One underlying issue = one case.

ServiceCase stores:

Guest
Room
Reason
Severity
Communication state
Time created
Time acknowledged
Resolution state

Do NOT generate duplicate cases for the same issue.

==================================================
12. SERVICE CASE LIFECYCLE
==================================================

Recommended states:

Uncommunicated

Guest notices problem,
but hotel does not know yet.

ContactingHotel

Phone ringing / guest walking to reception.

Communicated

Player now knows.

Acknowledged

Player explicitly acknowledged request.

Resolved

Underlying condition fixed.

Declined

Player declined a service request.

Escalated

Problem became serious.

This separates:

GUEST KNOWS

from:

HOTEL KNOWS.

That distinction is important.

==================================================
13. UI INFORMATION
==================================================

Before guest contacts hotel:

do NOT show full ServiceCase to normal player UI.

The guest may visibly behave differently,
but exact need remains hidden.

After communication:

show concise information.

Example:

ROOM 104

Guest says:
"It's still cold."

Do NOT show:

Cause = BoilerOutput 0.73
Solution = Extra Blanket.

Debug UI may show this information.

==================================================
14. GUEST BEHAVIORAL FEEDBACK
==================================================

Guests should subtly communicate discomfort before formally complaining.

Examples:

Cold:
guest interacts with radiator;
puts on coat if suitable animation exists;
returns to room activity.

Noise:
guest pauses activity;
looks toward source direction;
waits.

Do not spend large production time on animation.

Simple readable staging is enough.

==================================================
15. DO NOT OVER-ANIMATE
==================================================

Do not build a full animation system.

The goal is:

believable transitions.

Not cinematic realism.

Prioritize:

correct location;
correct timing;
correct world state.

==================================================
16. SERVICE FREQUENCY
==================================================

Do not increase total number of requests.

Potentially REDUCE it slightly.

Target:

a few meaningful guest contacts per day

rather than:

many shallow contacts.

A guest may complete a full stay without contacting staff.

This is desirable.

==================================================
17. RHYTHM OF A HOTEL DAY
==================================================

Different periods should naturally favor different types of interactions.

ARRIVAL WINDOW:

keys;
luggage;
room readiness;
initial requests.

EVENING:

temperature;
noise;
wake-up requests;
room problems.

NIGHT:

fewer soft service requests;
serious noise;
infrastructure incidents.

MORNING:

wake-up calls;
checkout;
late checkout;
room turnover.

Do not hardcode scripted event sequences.

Use schedule / context weighting.

==================================================
18. EXTRA BLANKET
==================================================

Preserve the existing blanket system.

Change when it becomes relevant.

Preferred flow:

Guest becomes mildly cold.

↓

Guest may increase radiator.

↓

Wait.

↓

If still mildly cold:
possible blanket request.

↓

If condition becomes severe:
temperature complaint.

Do not instantly request blanket when threshold changes.

==================================================
19. NOISE
==================================================

Preserve real-source requirement.

New preferred flow:

Noise begins.

↓

Guest notices.

↓

Guest tolerates for a while.

↓

If noise continues:
guest contacts hotel.

↓

Player investigates.

↓

Possible solutions remain:

ask source guest to quiet down;
move affected guest;
move noisy guest;
compensation;
ignore.

Do not create abstract noise requests.

==================================================
20. SELF-ADJUSTMENT CAN CREATE CONSEQUENCES
==================================================

This is desirable.

Example:

Several cold guests independently turn radiators higher.

↓

Boiler demand rises.

↓

Boiler efficiency begins dropping.

↓

Some rooms become colder.

This is emergent gameplay.

Do not script it as an event.

==================================================
21. PLAYER SHOULD SOMETIMES NOTICE FIRST
==================================================

Hotel management should not be entirely request-driven.

Examples:

Player hears loud TV in corridor
before complaint.

Player notices:
Circuit B load is high.

Player notices:
several radiators are set to maximum.

Player sees:
Room 104 needs preparation before arrival.

Player may intervene before anyone asks.

This should be rewarded naturally.

==================================================
22. SERVICE RESOLUTION
==================================================

Never resolve cases merely because player pressed a response button.

Underlying simulation must change.

Example:

Guest cold.

Player promises blanket.

Case NOT resolved yet.

Player actually delivers blanket.

Guest perception improves.

Now ServiceCase resolves.

==================================================
23. SERVICE COMMUNICATION DOES NOT EQUAL QUEST
==================================================

A phone call should give INFORMATION.

Not an objective arrow.

Example:

"It's quite cold."

Go to Linen Storage.
Pick up blanket.
Deliver to Room 104.

45. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — PAUSE
==================================================

Pause bug is considered fixed only when:

1. Root cause was identified from actual evidence.

2. Investigation is documented.

3. Fix specifically addresses the identified root cause.

4. Long pause can be reproduced safely after fix.

5. Resume does not cause a major simulation burst.

6. Gameplay state remains valid.

7. Solo still works.

8. LAN still works.

9. Fast-forward still works.

10. No speculative broad rewrite was performed without evidence.

==================================================
46. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — NATURAL SERVICE
==================================================

Natural Service pass is successful when:

1. Guests do not instantly contact hotel for every discomfort.

2. Guests can tolerate minor issues.

3. At least some guest self-help exists.

4. Self-help modifies actual world state.

5. Guests contact hotel through a physical/contextual channel.

6. Reception phone is functional.

7. Full request details are not revealed before communication.

8. ServiceCases are not duplicate-spammed.

9. Wake-up call requests occur at plausible moments.

10. Luggage requests occur only in relevant contexts.

11. Noise requests still require actual noise sources.

12. Extra blanket requests follow believable cold exposure.

13. Player is given the problem, not a prescribed solution.

14. Players can still proactively notice and solve problems.

15. A normal guest may complete a stay without making any request.

16. Three-day pacing remains active but feels less mechanical.

17. No new large gameplay subsystem was added.
