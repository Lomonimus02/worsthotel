==================================================
1. MAIN GOAL OF PROTOTYPE 0.2
==================================================

Prototype 0.1 revealed an important problem:

Once guests are accepted and checked in, the player can sometimes have nothing meaningful to do for approximately 1–2 minutes.

DO NOT solve this by adding random chores.

Do NOT fill empty time with:
- bring towel;
- carry luggage;
- clean random object;
- fetch item;
- take object from A to B;
- arbitrary NPC quests.

The goal is:

THE HOTEL MUST CONTINUE TO LIVE WITHOUT THE PLAYERS.

Guests should:
- arrive;
- move;
- use their rooms;
- perform activities;
- consume infrastructure;
- generate noise;
- affect temperature / power demand;
- react to their environment;
- interact indirectly with other guests.

The player's job is not:

"wait for a quest icon."

The player's job is:

OBSERVE
→ UNDERSTAND
→ PRIORITIZE
→ INTERVENE
→ ACCEPT CONSEQUENCES.

Core emotional target:

"We made this situation happen because of the decisions we made earlier."

This is critical.

DO NOT create gameplay like:

Instead:

WORLD STATE

This is the intended game.

6. SITUATION / ESCALATION SYSTEM
==================================================

Introduce or evolve the existing Incident / Request system into a more general:

SITUATION SYSTEM.

A Situation is not a quest.

It represents an unresolved mismatch between:
- guest needs;
- room state;
- infrastructure;
- other guests.

Example Situation:

ColdRoomSituation

Cause:
RoomTemperature below Guest minimum.

Stages could be:

Observed
Complaint
Escalated
Critical

Example:

0–20 seconds:
guest notices discomfort.

20–40 seconds:
guest contacts reception.

40–90 seconds unresolved:
strong satisfaction penalty.

Longer:
guest demands compensation
or considers leaving.

Exact timings must be configurable.

IMPORTANT:

For example:

- room becomes warm enough;
- guest moves to another acceptable room;
- guest checks out;

Do NOT require:

9. ELECTRICAL SYSTEM
==================================================

Add electricity as the second infrastructure system.

Do NOT make a full electrical engineering simulator.

CIRCUIT B:

Example:

Portable heater:
2 units.

Add heater:
5 units.

Circuit becomes overloaded.

Do not trip instantly.

Example:

- room condition penalties;
- some devices stop functioning;
- lighting changes visually.

This is very important.

The player must solve the cause,
not just spam reset.

Possible solutions:

- remove heater;
- move guest;
- accept cold room;
- reduce another load;
- reset after load falls.

18. HELD OBJECT PHYSICS TARGET
==================================================

Held objects should feel:

physical;
slightly heavy;
responsive;
stable;
damped.

They should NOT:

vibrate endlessly;
gain energy;
orbit the hold point;
explode when touching walls;
shake forever after camera movement.

Do not parent a dynamic Rigidbody directly to the camera.

Do not move dynamic Rigidbody using:

transform.position = ...

every frame.

Use either:

- properly configured ConfigurableJoint;

or

- a force / PD controller.

If using spring-like forces:

apply damping based on velocity difference.

General PD concept:

Force =
PositionError * PositionSpring
-
RelativeVelocity * PositionDamping

For angular rotation:

Torque =
RotationError * RotationSpring
-
AngularVelocity * AngularDamping.

Use critically or near-critically damped behavior.

A useful theoretical reference for a spring system:

critical damping approximately:

c = 2 * sqrt(k * m)

where:
c = damping
k = spring stiffness
m = rigidbody mass

Do not necessarily hardcode this exact formula blindly,
but use it to avoid underdamped infinite oscillation.

==================================================
43. PERFORMANCE
==================================================

This is a small prototype.

Do not prematurely optimize.

But avoid:

every guest performing expensive scene searches every Update;

FindObjectOfType in gameplay loops;

allocating large lists every simulation tick;

each guest independently querying entire hotel.

Use central systems where appropriate.

Guest needs can update at lower simulation frequency.

Example:
2–4 times per second is more than enough.

49. CORE PLAYTEST QUESTIONS
==================================================

After Prototype 0.2, the most important questions are:

Did players discuss where to place guests?

Did a decision from Planning Phase visibly matter later?

Did players ever say:

"We shouldn't have put those two next to each other"?

Did players ever solve one problem
and accidentally create another?

Did players choose between:

money,
guest happiness,
and infrastructure safety?

Did players ever intentionally ignore a complaint?

Did players use more than one solution to the same type of problem?

Did the hotel feel alive while the players were doing nothing?

Did players ever need to use WAIT because things were genuinely quiet,
rather than because the design simply ran out of content?

Did physical carrying feel stable and pleasant?

Most important:

Did the game create a story that was NOT explicitly scripted?

Example:

"We put the businessman beside the loud tourist.
Then the boiler started failing.
We gave Room 104 a heater.
That knocked out Circuit B.
Then the businessman lost power and got even angrier."

If this kind of story emerges from independent systems,
Prototype 0.2 is successful.

==================================================
50. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY REMINDER
==================================================

The Worst Hotel Ever must NOT become:

"Hotel-themed Overcooked with a list of chores."

It should become:

"A cooperative management simulation
where players deliberately run an old hotel too close to its limits,
then physically intervene when their decisions create cascading problems."

A physical action is good when it is the CONSEQUENCE of a meaningful decision.

Bad:

"The game told me to carry a heater."

Good:

"Room 104 is freezing.
Repairing the boiler is expensive.
We decided to use a heater.
Now we have to physically get it there.
And now the electrical circuit is overloaded."

Always protect this distinction.
